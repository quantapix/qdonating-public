## 5. One reader question, answered

> *(Seeded from Promise 2's live progress, not an inbound reader question.)*
> "Promise 2 says you're axiomatizing the U.S. Code in Lean. What did that
> actually look like this week — and how would an outside contributor plug in?"

Concretely: four new blocks of federal statute became machine-checked Lean this
week — parts of Title 18 (§§ 31–49) and Title 42 (§§ 201–217a-1) — each theorem
tied to a specific U.S.C. citation that resolves against a pinned corpus index,
so "we axiomatized it" is a claim you can re-run rather than take on faith. The
more important event was quieter: a 07-14 fix closed a hole where the Lean
kernel could accept a proof that silently proved nothing (an `autoImplicit`
phantom-type trap), and 17 files were rebuilt against the stricter setting. That
matters to a donor because the whole value of "LLM-predicate-backed theorems" is
that a green build *means* something; a build that can be green-but-empty is
worse than no proof. For an outside contributor, the surface is real and
entry-level: each un-axiomatized U.S.C. section is a self-contained unit of
work — pick a section, write the predicate spec against the canonical corpus
text, let the kernel be the referee. This is exactly the "opening to
contributors" the OSC re-application points at (§ 6). Added to the public FAQ at
`github.com/quantapix/qagents-public/discussions`.
