**Methodology note — the general lesson only.** The blockers were in the
rendered artifacts, not the sources, and most of them were invisible to reading.
A rendered page whose text has been converted to vector outlines carries no
extractable characters, so every text-based check over it is structurally blind
while still reporting success. The cure is to verify the rendered output against
its source directly — here, by comparing word multisets — because a page count
or a read-through passes layout defects that such a comparison catches. That is
a general result about verifying rendered output and it cost a day to learn.

## 5. One reader question, answered

> You said drafts were attacked by an adversarial loop instead of proofread.
> What did that teach you about the technique?

That the loop's design matters more than its effort, and a naive design does
damage of its own. If each cycle asks of every attacked sentence only *can this
be defended exactly as written?*, and cuts it when the answer is no, the loop
never asks the two questions that matter — whether the attack was actually
right, and whether only the *phrasing* was wrong. Two lessons generalise. **Audit
every iteration, not just the last**: a regression introduced early can survive
later rebuilds, so an audit that starts at the most recent iteration misses it.
And **a rewrite that keeps every citation is invisible to every mechanical
check**: citation counts and cite resolution can pass on text whose substance
has changed. The fix adopted was a standing constraint — only the operator may
withdraw an assertion — plus a restoration pass over every iteration. Both
halves are worth knowing before anyone copies the technique.
