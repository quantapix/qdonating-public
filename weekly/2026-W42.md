## 5. One reader question, answered

> *Seeded (no inbound this week):* "§ 3 counts 98 'attack theorems' on one
> axis. Why prove things against your own results?"

On the operational axis, each worked example has two parts. One is a proof
that a claimed property holds. The other is a set of attacks, written
separately, each a theorem aimed at a weak spot in the claim: a case it
misses, a counter-case, or a statement that holds only vacuously. A judge
then grades whether any attack actually defeats the proof. If one does, the
proof has to change, as happened to a W38 example. This week all three
examples were graded green. That is why attacks are counted apart from
proofs and never added to them as "results". A week with 94 proofs and
nothing tried against them would be weaker evidence than the same 94 with 98
attempts on record.
