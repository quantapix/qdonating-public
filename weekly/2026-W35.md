## 5. One reader question, answered

> *(Seeded from this week's own correction, and tied to live news rather than an
> inbound.)* If the drive can quietly overstate what donors' money buys for two
> and a half months, what exactly is the accountability claim worth?

It is worth what its failure mode shows, and this week is the failure mode
running as designed rather than an argument that it works. The overstatement was
real: from 2026-06-15 the drive's authoritative copy described a programmatic
lane inside its largest bucket as fully funded by an included credit pool, and
that pool had been withdrawn on that date. It sat wrong for ten weeks. Three
things are worth being precise about. **First, no money was involved** — inflow
has been zero every month since the drive opened, so nothing was misallocated,
and the error was an overstatement of what a subscription *includes*, not of what
was spent; the monthly ledgers show the zeros. **Second, it was caught by a
machine-checkable rule, not by good intentions** — the publishing pipeline
re-derives the public copy from source on every refresh and flags claims it
cannot substantiate, which is what filed this defect a week ago. **Third, and
least comfortable: the public page was corrected before the source document
was.** For seven days the redacted public copy was *more* accurate than the
authoritative one it renders from, which is exactly backwards and would have
silently regressed on the next refresh. That gap is what today closed. The honest
claim this drive makes is not "we do not get things wrong" — it is that being
wrong produces a dated, citable record with the correction attached, at the
source, on a published cadence. This paragraph is that record.
