## 5. One reader question, answered

> *Seeded (no inbound this week):* "§ 1 says two attack rounds 'landed
> nothing'. Isn't a round that finds nothing wasted work?"

No attack defeated a proof in either round, but the attackers still found
things. Their findings were against the prose, not the proofs: sentences
that said more than the proofs beneath them established. Each round conceded
those sentences and answered them in a new section of proofs. The earlier
section was left as it stood, so the record shows what was claimed and what
was corrected. The rounds kept finding the same kind of overstatement, so the
axis built a grader that compares a proof's description with its statement.
Measured at the time it was built, it raised two flags on the example, and a
hand read found both false. On older rounds it would have missed the
overstatements the attackers caught. It is report-only and gates nothing.
A round that lands nothing is the evidence that the proofs
held. The concessions show where the words had drifted from them.
