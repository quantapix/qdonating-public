## 5. One reader question, answered

> *Your largest bucket funds an AI subscription that has just been canceled,
> with access ending 2026-09-12. Isn't that the end of the drive — and if it is,
> why should anyone read next week's digest?*

It is not the end of the drive, and the distinction matters more than the
reassurance. What ended is a **resource**, on a known date. What the drive is,
structurally, has not moved: four exclusive-use buckets, a fiscal-sponsorship
custody model in which the developer never has account-level access to gross
inflow, and no salary, stipend, or honorarium leaving those buckets toward any
person. No donated money is implicated in any direction, because none was ever
received — the channel has been foreclosed since 2026-06-16 by the
Commonwealth's refusal of a government ID to someone whose only address is a
homeless-shelter P.O. box, and every lawful receiving instrument requires that
ID. Bucket 1 has never disbursed a donated dollar. There is no refund question
and no custody question here, because there is no money.

The honest part is the second half. The drive contract § 5.1 states that the
Max 20× tier is the floor for the three promises, not a preference, and that
every other deliverable runs on top of it — that recital was written to be
load-bearing, and it is load-bearing now, in the direction nobody wants. Through
2026-09-12 the work continues exactly as § 1 and § 3 show it continuing this
week. After 2026-09-12, absent re-subscription, the drive cannot restore that
tier from donations, because the drive still cannot receive any. Saying
otherwise would be the kind of claim this drive exists to avoid making.

So what should you do with next week's digest? Read it as the same instrument it
has always been: a dated record that reports what happened, including when what
happened was a loss. The drive's wind-down terms are already written down and are
not the author's judgment call — two consecutive missed weekly digests or any
missed monthly ledger breaks the *trivially auditable* contract by its own terms,
and the recovery obligation is a same-day disclosure explaining what broke. If
capacity after 2026-09-12 cannot sustain the cadence, that mechanism fires and
you will read about it here, on the day, rather than watching a page go quiet.
The August ledger is due **2026-09-05**, seven days before the access expiry, and
it will state the position as it stands on that date.

confirmation is an ordinary self-service cancellation notice and states no

What it does to the drive, precisely: **nothing structural, and something
material.** A drift-check written before this digest — the drive's drift-check
log, the 2026-08-14 entry — tests the event against every Bucket 1 recital in
the drive contract § 5 and § 5.1 and finds **no drift**, on four grounds. The
bucket is a definition of exclusive use for donated funds, not a representation
that a subscription is active. The finding that Max 20× is the floor for the
promised work is a claim about what the work requires, which losing access does
not lower. The "load-bearing" recital is what *creates* the duty to disclose
this, not something contradicted by it. And the drive told the record from the
outset that the subscription was carried by **short-term bridge funding outside
the drive**, with donor funding to take over "from its first funded renewal" —
inflow has been zero every month, so there has never *been* a first funded
renewal. A bridge described on the record as short-term reaching its end is that
recital operating as written. No supplemental affidavit is owed on
donation-drive grounds; the prior no-drift precedents of 2026-06-04 and
2026-07-17 are the governing shape. One sequencing fact is recorded in the audit
because it will matter later: the status report filed in the pending federal
appeal on 2026-08-13 was e-filed at 9:30:54 AM, **thirty-nine minutes before**
the cancellation confirmation, so the event is not inside that filed instrument.

What is material is **capacity, with a date on it**, and that is stated in § 5
