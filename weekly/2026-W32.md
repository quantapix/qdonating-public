## 5. One reader question, answered

> *You publish a "trivially auditable" contract and then miss your own
> five-day deadline for the July ledger. Why should anyone trust the numbers in
> a ledger that was late?*

Because the miss is reported by the same mechanism that reports everything
else, and because you can check that claim rather than take it. The drive's
cadence contract does not promise that nothing will slip; it promises that a
slip is disclosed on the record, same-day, with a cause. The July ledger posted
2026-08-07 against a 2026-08-05 target, and the first thing in it is a
disclosure saying so and setting out both halves of why.

The first half is a timeline, not an excuse. Five documents were e-filed in
three state trial-court dockets on 2026-07-31. One set was acted on the same
day; the other two were not resolved until 8/4 and 8/5, and closing that gap
took a call to the clerk's office on the afternoon of 8/5. A federal filing
whose record citations had to reflect that response was held for it and mailed
2026-08-04. This drive is one person, and that arc ran through the exact window
the July statement was due in.

The second half is not the court's, and it is the fixable one: the arc cleared
on 8/5 and the ledger still did not post on 8/6. The monthly statement, unlike
the weekly digest, has no scheduled trigger of its own — so nothing caught it in
the two days after the gate lifted. That is a real structural defect, it is
tracked as open work, and it is stated here rather than folded into the first
half, because an external cause does not absolve the part that was ours.

On the numbers themselves: lateness and inaccuracy are separate failures, and
only the first one happened. July inflow was **zero**, as it has been every
month, because the donation channel cannot lawfully receive funds at all — so
there was no figure whose two-day delay could have benefited anyone. Every
substantive claim in that ledger is a commit hash, a docket entry ID, or a
dated census stamp you can open yourself. The honest reading is not "the ledger
is unreliable" but "the ledger's *delivery* was unreliable once, and the drive
told you rather than hoping you would not check the date." If it happens twice
in a row, the drive's own wind-down terms say the contract is broken — that
condition is written down precisely so it is not the author's judgment call.

*Days six and seven are ours.* The arc cleared on 8/5 and the ledger still did
