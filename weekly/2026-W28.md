## 5. One reader question, answered

> "You publish your own redactions. What happens when the redaction is wrong — and
> how would a donor ever know?" (Seeded from this week's § 6 disclosure, not an
> inbound reader question.)

This week is the honest answer to that question, because the redaction *was*
wrong. The public mirror is produced by an automated redactor that strips state
dockets and personal information but deliberately preserves federal docket numbers
— a sensible default that, on 2026-07-03, carried three files into the public repo
containing federal collateral-matter references that a separate litigation-safety
rule bars from any public surface. Nothing about the automated gate caught it; a
human review two days later did. The fix was not just to delete the text: the
public repository's git *history* was rewritten to purge the material (a content
scrub alone only masks it, since the old blobs remain fetchable), a hard gate was
added to the publish pipeline so the same class of token cannot pass again, and
that gate ships with a 14-case regression test. Then every prior weekly digest and
the mirror were re-swept from scratch. A donor knows because it is written here,
in the digest, in the same week it happened, naming the failure rather than the
recovery — which is the entire point of a public cadence you cannot quietly skip.
Added to the public FAQ at
<https://github.com/quantapix/qagents-public/discussions>.
