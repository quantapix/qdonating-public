## 5. One reader question, answered

> Why open the U.S. Code axiomatization to outside contributors now —
> doesn't a "single developer + AI" project lose its point if others join?

The point was never that one person did it alone; the point is that the work
is **trivially verifiable** — every theorem cites canonical U.S. Code text and
every predicate ships as a checkable artifact. That property gets stronger,
not weaker, with more hands: a contributor picking up a section is one more
independent reader who can falsify a claim. The project today is a single
developer working with AI assistance, and the invitation opens contribution
without misrepresenting it as already collaborative. The axiomatization works
over public federal statutes only — no private material is ever involved — so
it carries no privacy-floor surface and is the natural place to build in the
open. Start at `qnarre-public` (CONTRIBUTING + good-first-issues) and the
`qagents-public` Discussions.

## 6. Donation-channel disclosure — OSC declined; holding + re-applying + opening to contributors

This is a same-day disclosure note. The drive's whole pitch is *trivially
verifiable*; that means the channel news is reported plainly, on its own date,
good or bad.

**What happened.** The fiscal-sponsorship application to **Open Source
Collective** (submitted 2026-05-23) was **declined on 2026-06-03**. OSC's
stated reason: its fiscal-hosting model is built for community-driven projects
with active collaboration and usage, and a solo, early-stage project may not
yet have the level of community involvement that structure needs. OSC
expressly invited a re-application as the project grows and more contributors
get involved.

**What this does not change.** The drive's work opened on schedule 2026-06-01
and continues unchanged: the four exclusive-use buckets, the $1,800
fixed-bucket floor, the 6/1 → 12/1 window, the three deliverable promises, and
the redacted-input privacy floor all stand. The donation channel was already
disclosed in advance as floating until OSC approves; a decline extends that
float, it does not alter the structure. No donor has been or will be charged
before the channel is live; no funds sit in limbo.

**The response — turn the objection into the work.** OSC's objection is about
community involvement, not output. So the answer is to open the biggest
public-benefit effort to outside contributors: help axiomatize the U.S. Code,
in the age of AI. The framework is building Lean4 theorems — backed by
LLM-evaluated predicates — over the United States Code (full corpus mirrored;
~65,700 sections across all 54 titles), over public federal statutes only.
Lean4 users, formal-methods people, legal-tech builders, and the
AI-assisted-software-curious are invited to pick up a section, a predicate, or
a title. Start at `qnarre-public` and the project Discussions.

The project today remains a single developer working with AI assistance — the
invitation opens contribution; it does not represent the work as already
collaborative.

**Timeline.** The drive re-applies to OSC at end of June with the built-up
public record — weekly digests, published repos, the first explainer videos on
the @Quantapix YouTube channel, and early contributor traction on the
axiomatization. The channel stays float-pending until then; the exact go-live
date is disclosed in the weekly digest of the week the channel actually starts
receiving funds.
